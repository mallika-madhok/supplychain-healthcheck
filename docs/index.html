Methodology

        
What the numbers mean, where they come from, and what they can't tell you.


        
What this is for

        
Three things you don't control can move your costs: how hard it is to ship goods
        around the world, the Canadian dollar against the US dollar, and how much of your
        buying depends on a few countries. This page puts a plain reading on each, translates
        the currency one into dollars, and points to what in your business is worth checking. It does not tell you what to do: it doesn't know your contracts, margins or suppliers, and you do. It does not forecast
        your costs, and it says nothing about any specific supplier or brand.


        
What you enter

        
The product you mostly buy, your monthly purchases, the share of those purchases
        paid in US dollars, and roughly what share comes from each country. These are
        estimates you give, not data we collect, and they stay in your browser and the page
        address.


        
How the status labels are decided

        
Each signal is compared with its own history and given a z-score: how far
        today is from its average, measured in standard deviations (the size of a normal
        up-and-down for that signal). Around 0 is average, up to 1 is within the normal range, 1 to 2 is
        unusual, 2 to 3 is rare, and 3 or more means something is really off.
        Below 1 is shown as Normal, 1 to 2 as Worse than usual, and above 2 as
        Much worse than usual. These cut-offs are a first-pass choice and haven't been tested
        against real price changes yet.


        
The three signals

        
    
          
  • Shipping. The New York Fed's Global Supply Chain Pressure Index, which tracks
          how much it costs to ship goods and how long deliveries are taking around the world. Today is
          compared with the full record since 1998. A shorter record would be dominated by the
          2021–22 crisis, which would make bad conditions look normal. The "worse than X% of months"
          line covers the last 10 years, and "last 3 months" shows whether it is getting better or worse.
          This is worldwide, so it is the same for every business.
    • 
          
  • Currency. Two checks on the US/Canadian dollar rate, each compared with
          history since 2017 and averaged: how much the rate has changed over the last 30 business days
          (about six weeks), and how much it has gone up and down over the last 90 (about four months).
          The dollar figures are simple arithmetic: your monthly purchases times the share paid in US
          dollars, times the change in the rate. "Normal change over six weeks" is the standard deviation
          of the six-week change since 2017.
    • 
          
  • Suppliers. A reliance score for your country mix: square each country's
          share and add them up (one country at 100% scores 1, an even spread scores close to 0). It is
          compared with the same score for Canada's imports across all clothing and footwear product
          lines over the last 12 months. "Other" counts as spread across many suppliers, so it adds
          nothing.
    •

Limits to keep in mind

        
    
          
  • Dollar figures assume you keep buying the same amount and pay today's exchange rate.
          If you have locked in a rate with your bank, or your supplier changes its prices when the
          rate moves, your real numbers will differ.
    • 
          
  • Country means where customs says the goods were made, which is not always where
          they were sewn.
    • 
          
  • About 0.0001% of import value has no country recorded and is left out of the
          reliance figures.
    • 
          
  • Past history is a guide to what is unusual, not a forecast. This is not financial
          advice.
    • 
        


        
How this page works

        
There is no server. A weekly job downloads the latest data, recalculates it and
        publishes small data files next to this page. Your browser loads them with
        DuckDB-Wasm
        and runs the SQL you can open under each chart, right on your device.
